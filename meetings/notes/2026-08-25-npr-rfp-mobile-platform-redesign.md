# NPR: Next-Generation Mobile Platform Redesign (RFP intro)

**Date:** August 25, 2026
**Participants:** Matt New (Uptech); Michael Seifollahi, Laura Galbraith, Erica Osher, Kahlia Ali, Monica Ostolaza, Joel Sucherman (NPR)
**Source:** Granola (db5f8780-192a-4925-b5c9-e044cba78740)
**Deal:** `NPR - New Deal`

## NPR's vision and audience goals

- Primary goal: **5x weekly active users**
- Three audience segments:
  - Existing radio listeners migrating to digital (older, loyal public radio audience)
  - "Backseat kids" who grew up with public radio but lost that passive exposure
  - New audiences arriving through podcasts, Tiny Desk, etc.
- Core challenge: be the right thing for each person, not all things to everyone
- App's "final form": a home for members who are deep supporters. Ticket giveaways, live events, NPR+ exclusives. Membership as the destination

## Product direction and scope

- Wants a genuine blue-sky rethink, not a fresh coat of paint
  - Existing tools: personalized on-demand, live streaming, playlist/likes, CDS content API, identity service
  - Open to new APIs and services to power unconceived experiences
- Merged two apps into one; now want to think beyond that combined frame
- NPR's superpower: seamless national/local content mix (works on radio, harder digitally)
- **Station app consolidation in scope:** ~80-100 member stations paying for low-ROI apps. NPR app as aggregator could replace many
  - Open question: co-branding, multi-station markets (KCRW + KPCC in SoCal)
- ~400-500 person newsroom, 17 global bureaus, 35+ podcasts, NPR Music
- No existing influencer/ambassador infrastructure, but open to exploring

## Team structure and engagement model

- NPR in-house mobile team (iOS, Android, smart speakers/screens) stays in place
- Backend: CDS, personalization services, push notifications, identity
- Design team is small and scrappy; **expects agency to do ~99% of design work**
  - Deliverable: prototype plus design system components handed to the internal team post-project
- Three bid tracks: discovery and design only, build only, or both
- Preferred model leans toward blue-sky discovery first, then evaluate build path
- Code review and knowledge transfer expected so the internal team can maintain and iterate

## Open questions

- Pricing/scoping challenge acknowledged: Track A outcomes will heavily shape Track B estimates
- Uptech's model (bi-weekly data reviews, iterative experiments, ongoing partnership) resonated
- How far does "reimagination" go beyond digital (events, community, ambassadors)?
- How does co-branding work across multi-station markets?
- What content, APIs, and services are actually available to build on?
