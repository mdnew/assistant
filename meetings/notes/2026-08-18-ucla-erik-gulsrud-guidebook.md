# UCLA / Gulsrud Consulting: parent guidebook to digital product

**Date:** August 18, 2026
**Participants:** Matt New, Claude Ciocan (Uptech); Erik Gulsrud (Gulsrud Consulting), Kasari (UCLA GSEIS), A Gulsrud (UCLA Mednet)
**Source:** Granola (84b995c7-362f-4550-94ea-79998a4dd867)

## Project vision and MVP thinking

- Erik's framing: focus on a minimum viable product before scaling
  - Stage 1 only, not everything at once
  - LMS platform as a low-barrier first step: reimagine the existing guidebook in that format
  - Claude surfaced a lead for a "bare bones" guide-style vendor
- Key early question: would parents pay for this, and in what format?
  - Test with families first: username/password access, gather feedback on usefulness and willingness to pay (~$10?)

## Content strategy and format

- Core challenge: content is heavy and complex; parents are often overwhelmed
  - Kasari: every child is different, need flexibility in how principles are presented
  - Matt: parents trust clinical voices but may not sit down to read long-form content
- Format ideas: simple booklet/ebook tool (~$50/month, self-serve, supports paywall); break the existing PDF guidebook into smaller chunks; animations, video, interactive elements; light-touch daily prompts (Duolingo-style engagement, not gamification for its own sake)
- Agreed: pick one learning pattern, do it well, then expand

## User research as a foundation

- Matt's strong recommendation: do user research before building
  - Understand parents' mental models, how they consume information, where they are emotionally
  - Build personas around both parent and child profiles
  - Referenced the SDS project: deep interviews with teachers and students shaped the build
- Risk: building without research risks producing something that does not fit how parents actually engage

## Potential collaboration levels

1. Consumer research: interviews and data gathering on what parents actually want
2. Build support: help format and deliver content (ebook tool, or full custom build later)

Scaling path: start with a simple booklet tool, wrap into a larger site/app as the product grows. Future features could include video uploads with therapist feedback.
