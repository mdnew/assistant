How many of you present success metrics in your kickoff meetings?

Not at the end of the project when someone asks how it went. At the beginning, on a slide, before anyone writes a ticket.

I did not do this for a long time. We would scope the work, build it, ship it, and then go looking for numbers to explain what happened. You can always find a number that makes a launch look fine. That is the problem.

Now we write the number down first. On a feature we are shipping ahead of the November election, we agreed on what success looks like before the first ticket existed. Target installs for the week. A notification opt-in rate we want to reach. An active user count the app has never hit. One of those is genuinely ambitious, and we said so out loud.

The part that surprised me is what it does for the team, not for me.

When a target is just a number, it feels like pressure. When you tie it back to the client's OKR, it becomes context. The team stops asking what we are building and starts asking why this and not the other three things on the list. A developer who knows the goal is opt-in rate will tell you the toggle is buried in the wrong screen. That conversation does not happen when the goal is "ship the feature."

It also makes cutting scope easier. If something does not move the number in the OKR, it is a nice to have, and everyone can see that for themselves.

Half the value of a success metric is knowing whether you won. The other half is telling your team why the work matters before they start.

Do you set the target in the kickoff, or after launch?
