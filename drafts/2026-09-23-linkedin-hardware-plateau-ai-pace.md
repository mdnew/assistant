I gave my daughter my iPhone 15 Pro this week. I had it for three years. She was still on a 13 Pro.

So we sat down and went through what actually changed between her old phone and my new 18 Pro. Five model years. Two extra buttons, the Dynamic Island, and a different charging cable.

That is the list.

I remember when an iPhone announcement was an event. You found a stream, you watched the whole thing, and there was always one moment where the room genuinely reacted.

This year I have updated my watch, my laptop, and my phone. All three. Nothing feels different. Everything is faster in ways I do not notice and better in ways I cannot describe.

Meanwhile the way I actually do my job has changed more in the last eighteen months than in the ten years before it. I write acceptance criteria differently. I get much further into a ticket before it reaches a developer. The developers spend their time directing outcomes instead of typing out the obvious parts. None of that needed new hardware. It showed up on the machines we already had.

That is a strange inversion if you have been doing this a while. Hardware used to set the pace. You waited for the device that made the thing possible, and your roadmap organized itself around release cycles you did not control.

Now the hardware is good enough that it got out of the way, and the pace is set by software, which has no keynote and does not wait for September.

I do not think that is worse. It is just the first time in a long while that the phone in my pocket is the least interesting thing that changed this year.

Anyone else upgrade everything this year and feel almost nothing?
